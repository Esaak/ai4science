# 6. CONCLUSION (с. 13–15)

[с. 13]
6. CONCLUSION
In this study, we presented the fast convolutional surrogate neural model for the steady-state
PM2.5 field of an idealized urban canopy that satisfies standard dispersion-model acceptance cri­
teria (𝑅2 = 0.86,F AC2 = 0.75,NMSE = 0.44). We demonstrate that the predicted PM2.5 field
is governed primarily by the emission sources and vegetation configuration. Also, a controlled
ablation study shows that data augmentation is essential whereas explicit wind conditioning
(FiLM) gives no measurable benefit in prediction quality and slightly worsens the mass-balance
metric. We intend to explore the alternative options for loss formulations since we see the
log-space loss being the main limitation in the current problem formulation.
