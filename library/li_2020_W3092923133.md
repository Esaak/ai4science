# Fourier Neural Operator for Parametric Partial Differential Equations

## Аннотация

[источник] A notebook-first data science project asking a deliberately narrow question: when does a Fourier Neural Operator (FNO) provide forecast information beyond persistence for real sea-surface temperature fields, and at which spatial scales does that gain occur? The study uses NOAA/NCEI Optimum Interpolation Sea Surface Temperature (OISST) v2.1 over the Northeast Atlantic (roughly 30-50°N, 30-5°W) and evaluates 7-day-ahead field forecasting from the previous 14 daily SST fields. Training covers 2020-2024, validation 2025, and testing a frozen later holdout period. Splits are strictly temporal and preprocessing is fit on training data only. The FNO is benchmarked against a hierarchy of baselines rather than reported in isolation: train-only seasonal climatology, persistence (the primary null model), linear trend, a global low-rank EOF+ridge linear dynamics model, and a small local CNN. Evaluation reports masked RMSE, MAE, anomaly correlation, persistence skill, paired daily RMSE differences with block-bootstrap uncertainty, spatial error maps, and scale-resolved spectral error energy, so that aggregate gains cannot hide regional failures or high-frequency smoothing. Scope and limitations are stated explicitly. The repository claims no novel neural-operator architecture and no first application of FNOs to ocean or Atlantic SST forecasting; the contribution is the diagnostic design that attributes skill by spatial frequency band, season, and validation-defined forecast-difficulty regime. Because OISST v2.1 is a bias-adjusted, optimally interpolated Level-4 analysis, forecasting OISST is not equivalent to forecasting unprocessed ocean observations, and part of its apparent spatial predictability may originate in the analysis pipeline. This is documented as a threat to validity, alongside the mask and taper caveats that constrain spectral interpretation. A negative result - no robust skill over persistence - is treated as a publishable outcome. Contents: eleven sequenced Jupyter notebooks (problem framing, data acquisition via NOAA ERDDAP, audit and EDA, spectral analysis, leakage-safe supervised dataset construction, baselines, FNO architecture, training, frozen test evaluation, spectral skill attribution, robustness and ablation, conclusions and model card); a tested oisst_fno Python package with data, baseline, model, and metric utilities; and documentation covering research positioning, data card, provenance and provenance audit, threats to validity, and references.
