Abstract 
In recent years, rapid urbanization has significantly impacted water quality in urban areas. A ccurate 
prediction models for pollutant dispersion play a pivotal role in enhancing our ability to respond 
effectively to pollution incidents and promote sustainable urban development. However, the 
computational cost brought by traditional simulation models is relatively high and time -consuming. To 
address this problem, surrogate models are employed to enable efficient and rapid evaluations. This 
paper proposes a simulation -based prediction method for ri ver water quality. First, a one -dimensional 
hydrodynamic water quality model is established by HEC -RAS. The riverbed data is derived through 
cubic spline difference, facilitating the simulation of the water pollutant transport process. Secondly, the 
hydraulic and water quality simulation results are obtained from the model constructed above. 
Subsequently, the simulation results are used as input to a deep neural network for further training and 
learning processes. By evaluating the applicability of deep learning models in predicting water pollution, 
this method can provide a valuable reference for urban water governance.

1. Introduction 
The rapid pace of modernization has give n rise to a multitude of environmental challenges, with water 
resource pollution being a prominent concern. To enhance the ability to address pollution incidents 
effectively, it is imperative to investigate the pollutant dispersion patterns in urban rivers to effectively 
predict key water quality indicators. Pollutant dispersion simulation methods mainly include data-driven 
modeling methods and physic -based methods according to dispersion mechanisms, such as using 
cellular automata to simulate the dispersion process of water pollutants or establishing an instantaneous 
point source pollution model to dynamically simulate the ch anges in river pollutant concentration over 
time[1]. In contrast to data-driven models, widely employed simulation software such as MIKE21, QUAL-
II, and WASP offer rapid and precise simulations of the pollut ant migration process[2]. The Hydrologic 
Engineering Center's River Analysis System (HEC-RAS) model, originally developed for the U.S. Army 
Corps of Engineers, is capable of performing steady -state and unsteady -state one-dimensional water 
quality simulation and two -dimensional hydrodynamic calculations [3]. Consequently, this study adopts 
the HEC-RAS framework for conducting simulation-based research on the pollutant dispersion process 
in urban rivers. 
Physic-based simulations of pollutant dispersion in urban rivers necessitate a substantial quantity of 
boundary parameters derived from practical scenarios, which generally results in significant 
computational costs. In recent years, deep learning (DL) has emerged as a promising approach to serve 
as a surrogate model to address complex environmental challenges [4], [5]. In this study, we apply DL 
techniques to conduct hydrodynamic modeling and water quality assessment. HEC -RAS is first

employed to construct a physic-based hydrodynamic and pollutant dispersion model. Subsequently, the 
data obtained from the HEC-RAS model is input into a deep neural network (DNN) for training. As a 
surrogate model, DNN is able to predict pollutant concentrations at different profiles and reduce the 
required calculation time while maintaining a better accuracy performance.

2.3. Deep Neural Network Models 
The construction of multiple hidden layers and the training data are the keys to improve the performance 
of the DNN model (Figure 1). DNN has nonlinear mapping capabilities and is commonly used to analyze 
complex nonlinear problems [6]. Three types of layers (i.e., input layer, output layer, and hidden layer) 
are contained in a DNN structure . These layers are fully interconnected, and information propagation 
between them adheres to the formula y = ωx + b and an activation function. Each connection has a 
unique weight (ω) and bias (b). Activation functions play a pivotal role in capturing nonlinear 
relationships among variables. Commonly used activation functions include the Sigmoid and Relu 
functions. This study applies DNN networks to the river pollutant dispersion simulation to achieve rapid 
prediction of water quality.

2.4. Boundary conditions 
The Xiantao segment of Hanjiang River was selected as the study area. The upper reaches of the river 
are 300 meters away from the Tianmen Bridge, while the lower reaches are in close proximity to Shiji 
Village. To establish a comprehensive understanding of the river’s topography, we utilized digital 
elevation model (DEM) data with a resolution of 12.5 m from publicly available datasets. Channel 
centerlines, shorelines, and basin lines are added based on the chann el outline. Additionally, we 
positioned profiles along the river at intervals ranging from 500 to 1000 meters. These profiles were 
essential for capturing the intricate terrain variations within the river channel. Notably, due to the 
challenging topography and the need to account for reduced spacing between profiles at bends and 
turns, a total of 80 profiles were obtained. The profile IDs span a range from 100 (minimum) to 67000 
(maximum), providing a comprehensive dataset for further analysis. The river profile is shown in Figure 
2.

3.Results and discussion 
In this section, the authors conduct hydrodynamic simulation using HEC -RAS, and the simulated data 
is then imported into a DNN for training and validation. 
3.1. Simulation results of hydrodynamic model 
The simulation time interval is defined from November 30, 2021, to December 15, 2021, with a 
simulation time step of one day. The upstream inflow profile is identified by profile ID 67000, while the 
downstream outflow profile corresponds to profile ID 100. Profile ID 11291 downstream is selected for 
model validation. The upstream boundary condition is specified using the water level, and the 
downstream boundary condition is set to normal depth. The initial conditions were set at a still water 
start. As depic ted in Figure 4, the hydrodynamic simulation exhibits a maximum error of 1.60% on 
December 13th, with the exception of the second day, which incurred an error of 7.15% . The high 
accuracy of the hydrodynamic simulation has established a foundation for subsequent water quality 
simulations.

3.2. Simulation results of pollutant dispersion model 
Pollutant dispersion simulation is conducted based on higher hydrodynamic simulation accuracy. The 
main water quality factors in the Xiantao segment of the Hanjiang River include permanganate, ammonia 
nitrogen (NH 3-N), total phosphorus (TP), and dissolved oxygen (DO). Leveraging available data, we 
specifically focus on permanganate and TP for detailed simulation analysis and the missing data were 
completed using linear interpolation. At the same time, due to the mismatch between the actual water 
quality and the hydrodynamic measurement time, a reduction facto r is applied to rectify the pollutant 
simulation process. The modeling framework integrates critical parameters, including water quality data, 
meteorological datasets, dispersion coefficients, and initial conditions. These inputs are incorporated 
into the NSMI module within the HEC-RAS software, facilitating the construction of an initial water quality 
model. The simulation time step is set at 15 minutes to capture dynamic variations . The simulation 
results are shown in Figure 5. The maximum error has been reduced to 6.35% as shown in Table 1.

3.3. Analysis of DNN prediction results 
To obtain the training data for the DNN model, the HEC -RAS model was used to simulate different 
pollutant concentrations occurring in the upper reaches of the river profile, thereby obtaining 
concentration data of water quality indicators in the downstream profile. The DNN model was 
implemented using the Python programming language. Its input parameters consist of permanganate 
concentrations from different upstream profil es during a pollution event. The desired output is the 
permanganate concentration corresponding to downstream profile ID 45116. To optimize the model's 
performance, a K -fold cross -validation approach was employed to determine the optimal number of 
hidden layers. As was shown in Figure 6, the highest average R2 value of 0.983 was obtained with a 
configuration of two hidden layers, each comprising six neurons. Simultaneously, the root mean square 
error (RMSE) was 0.117, indicating a relatively low level of predictive error . In general, the DNN model 
demonstrates high prediction accuracy in predicting water quality in the specified region.

4. Conclusion 
In this research, the HEC -RAS software is employed to establish hydrodynamic and water quality 
models for the Xiantao profile of the Hanjiang River. Specifically, we conduct one-dimensional unsteady 
flow simulations and pollutant dispersion process simulations, focusing on the dispersion process of 
permanganate and TP. The maximum error in pollutant dispersion simulation is 6.35%, indicating a high 
level of prediction accuracy. To overcome the challenge of the high computing cost of physic -based 
models, we integrate the HEC-RAS model with DNN. The DNN is trained using data obtained from the 
HEC-RAS simulations, and K-fold cross-validation is performed to assess model performance. The DNN 
exhibited optimal R 2 performance of 0.98 under the parameter setting of two hidden layers and six 
neurons, which verifies the satisfactory prediction accuracy of the model. Consequently, the DNN model 
can serve as a surrogate model for the physic-based model to predict water pollutant concentrations in 
the specified region, thereby providing valuable insights for local water quality management.
