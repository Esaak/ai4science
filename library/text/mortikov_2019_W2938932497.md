Abstract:The paper presents the results of direct numerical simulation of turbulent plane Couette flow. The
calculations were performed for Reynolds numbers Re=U0H/ν (H is the height of the channel,±U0/2is the
motion velocities of the lower and upper walls, respectively,ν is the kinematic viscosity) from5200 (where
viscouseffectssignificantlyaffecttheflowstructure)to 80000(wherealogarithmiclayerisclearlyobserved).
EstimatesoftermsoftheequationforthebalanceofturbulentReynoldsstressesareobtained,whichindicate
the importance of the kinetic energy transport by velocity fluctuations. The vertical transport of the turbu-
lent momentum flux is less important and partly compensated by the transport of pressure fluctuations. It is
shownthatinthelogarithmiclayerthenormalcomponentsofthe‘pressure–strainrate’correlationtensorare
describedintheframeworkofthe‘isotropizationofproduction’model,andinthecentralpartofthechannel
they are described by the linear Rotta model [29]. The additive model considering both the interaction of the
velocityfieldfluctuationandtheinfluenceofthemeanvelocitygradientisagoodapproximationonlyforthe
off-diagonal component of the tensor entering the balance equation for the turbulent momentum flux.
Keywords:Couette flow, turbulence, direct numerical simulation.

The Couette flow is a flow of a viscous fluid between two infinite plane parallel walls moving relative to each
otherintheabsenceofpressuregradient.ThisflowislinearlystableforallReynoldsnumbers[28].However,
transition to turbulence occurs in the laboratory experiments even for Re≈ 1300 [8]. The formulation of the
problem assumes that the flow is homogeneous in horizontal direction and the total flux of the streamwise
momentumcomponentisconstantatanydistancefromthewall.Theconstancyofthestress τ(z) =τ0 =ρu 2τ
(uτ is the friction velocity,ρ is the fluid density) allows us to assume from dimensionality reasons that there
is a universal form of dependenceu+ = f(z+) involving dimensionless variables, i.e., the mean flow velocity
u+ = u/uτ and the distance from the wallz+ = zuτ/ν, and also, according to the classic Prandtl theory, the
existenceofalogarithmiclayer.Notethatthecondition τ ≈ constistypical,inparticular,forthesurfacelayer
of the atmosphere at altitudes up toHa ≈ 50 meters. In this respect, the Couette flow can be considered as a
model problem for studying characteristics and structure of shear turbulence in the absence of the thermal
stratificationandforcesofrotation,verificationofsemi-empiricaltheoriesandparameterizationsofturbulent
processes.
Investigation of turbulent Couette flow in the laboratory [3, 4, 9, 12, 31] presents a number of technical
problems, in particular, for largeRe and hence for large movement velocitiesU0 of walls. Therefore, the
experiments were performed for relatively small Reynolds numbers, for example, for Re≈ 20000, see [12].
Thedifficultyofperforminglaboratorymeasurementsleadstodiscrepanciesevenintheevaluationofintegral
flow characteristics such as the friction coefficient [12].

4 Conclusion
Inthispaper,wepresenttheresultsofdirectnumericalsimulationofaturbulentCouetteflowwithReynolds
numbers up to Re= 80000. The calculations show the formation of a logarithmic layer for the mean stream-
wisevelocitycomponentforRe τ ≈ 1900.Inspiteofthefactthatthisresultisconsistentwithclassicconcepts
ofexistenceofalogarithmiclayerundertheconditionthatthetotalmomentumfluxisconstantanddoesnot
depend on the distance from the wall, it requires additional verification, i.e., experimental studies with in-
creasing horizontal sizes of the domain and large Reynolds numbers, careful investigation of the sensitivity
of results to the grid resolution.
The presence of large-scale structures in the Couette flow leads to the comparability of the turbulent
diffusionofthekineticenergywiththeshearproductionofturbulenceinthecentralpartofthechannel.Inthe
balanceequationfortheverticalmomentumflux,theturbulentdiffusionislesssignificantandtheturbulent
transport of momentum fluxu󸀠w󸀠 is compensated by the transport of pressure fluctuations. The terms of the
equationfortheReynoldsstresstensorgenerallyshowarelativelyweakdependenceontheReynoldsnumber
for Reτ ⩾ 1000, which allows us to use the direct numerical simulation data to test the applicability of semi-
empiricalsecondorderturbulenceclosuresassumingRe ≫ 1.However,thevelocityfluctuationintensitiesare
clearlysensitiveto Re andforsufficientlylargeReynoldsnumbersweobserveformationofasecondarylocal
maximumfor (u󸀠)+ locatedinthelogarithmiclayer,whichmayindicatethepresenceofatransientregimein
the turbulent Couette flow for1500 < Reτ < 2500.
The modelling results show that normal components of ‘pressure–strain rate’ correlations can be repre-
sented in the logarithmic region by the model of ‘isotropization of production’ withCIP ≈ 0.9. At the center
of the channel the diagonal terms ofRij are sufficiently well approximated by the linear Rotta model with
CR ≈ 1.8 describing the return of the turbulence to isotropy. Additive models of form (3.6) based on rep-
resentation of two-point correlations in the form of one-point moments may introduce significant errors to
the parameterization of ‘pressure–strain rate’ correlations. The importance of representation ofRij in the
second order turbulence models is also determined by the error associated with the modelling of dissipa-
tiveprocesses.Thedevelopmentofcorrectclosuresfor‘pressure–strainrate’correlationsandmodelsforthe
dissipation rateεij seems relevant for improving turbulent closures.
